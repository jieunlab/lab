WHAT WE DO

Research

Catalytic Systems for Artificial Photosynthesis and Sustainable Energy Conversion

Our research focuses on the development of molecular catalytic systems for sustainable energy conversion and chemical transformations. By integrating transition-metal complex design, photochemistry, electrochemistry, and mechanistic investigations, we aim to convert abundant resources such as CO2 and H2O into valuable fuels and chemicals.

01

Artificial Photosynthesis & Photoredox Chemistry

We develop artificial photosynthetic and photoredox systems inspired by natural photosynthesis. Our research explores water oxidation, oxygen activation, and light-driven chemical transformations using molecular metal catalysts, including sustainable H2O2 production from H2O and O2.

02

Molecular Catalyst Design for CO2 Reduction

We design robust transition-metal complexes with tailored ligand environments for selective CO2 reduction. In particular, PNNP-ligated Ir, Ru, and Os catalysts enable efficient and durable photocatalytic conversion of CO2 into formic acid and CO, providing insights into structure–activity relationships.

03

Photo- & Electrocatalytic Energy Conversion

We integrate molecular catalysts into light-driven and electrochemical systems for sustainable fuel production. Our studies include catalyst immobilization on electrode surfaces, selective CO2-to-formate conversion in aqueous media, and light-driven hydrogenation of CO2 and carbonyl compounds under mild conditions.

04

Mechanistic Studies & Spectroscopy

We investigate catalytic reaction mechanisms through time-resolved spectroscopy, electrochemistry, kinetic analysis, and computational studies. By identifying reactive intermediates and elucidating electron-, proton-, and hydrogen-atom transfer pathways, we establish molecular-level principles for improving catalytic activity, selectivity, and durability.
